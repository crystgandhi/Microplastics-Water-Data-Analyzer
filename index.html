Case studies on capturing and processing microplastics in water and wastewater.

Ceramic microfiltration for industrial laundry wastewater

Study scale
Eight-week pilot using industrial laundry wastewater
Reported result
More than 97% removal of microplastic fibers. The study reported an influent concentration of 38,616 ± 1,140 fibers per liter.
What happens after capture?
Filtration separates fibers from the water into a concentrated residue that requires disposal or further processing.

Magnetic ferrofluid capture · Mia Heller

Study scale
Student prototype presented at the 2025 Regeneron International Science and Engineering Fair.
How it works
An oil-based liquid containing tiny magnetic particles is mixed into the water. Microplastics associate with the oil. A magnetic separator and a layered filter then separate the captured plastic and recover ferrofluid for reuse.
Microplastic removal
95.52% under the prototype’s test conditions.
Ferrofluid recovery
87.15% of the ferrofluid used was recovered.
What happens after capture?
The system separates the captured plastic from the water and recirculates the recovered ferrofluid.

Ultrasonic microplastic filtration · Justin Huang and Victoria Ou

Study scale
Student laboratory prototypes presented at Regeneron ISEF in 2024 and 2025.
How it works
Piezoelectric transducers generate ultrasound in a tube. The acoustic force blocks suspended particles as water flows through.
2024 laboratory tests
Polyethylene removal was 84–94% with one stage and 94–96% with two stages at flow rates of 10–40 mL/min.
2025 follow-up
The students tested polyethylene and polystyrene particles in the 20–200 µm size range. In polyethylene tests spanning 40–300 mL/min, one two-stage configuration reached up to 96.5% removal.
What happens after capture?
The 2025 prototype included an automated particle retrieval and cleaning system.
